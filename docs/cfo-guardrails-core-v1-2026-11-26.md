## 1. Rules already agreed

1. **Use the right business and evidence.** Figures and commentary must refer to the same company, currency, reporting dates, store timezone and evidence version. Switching business or losing access clears the previous business's results.
2. **Keep facts, examples and assumptions distinct.** Reviewed synthetic-company records are fictional business evidence. Design examples are illustrations. Neither is a real Shopify, Xero, advertising or bank observation.
3. **Missing is not zero.** No missing cost, advertising, customer, balance or comparison may silently become zero or an example amount. A verified zero requires sufficient coverage.
4. **Keep supported parts visible.** Missing overheads need not hide valid sales, gross profit or contribution. Withhold the results that depend on the missing inputs.
5. **Keep calculations consistent.** Commentary uses the shared financial results; the language model must not invent a competing calculation. Every cost is counted once.
6. **Use actual event dates.** Original paid/completed orders remain orders after later refunds. Exclude unpaid, test and pre-sale-cancelled orders. Sales, refunds and saleable-stock recoveries belong to their respective event periods; do not rewrite history to make a comparison look better.
7. **Use supported historical costs and recorded tax.** No current-price substitution for missing historical cost; no blanket VAT assumption. VAT is kept separate and not deducted twice.
8. **Respect missing or stale evidence.** Keep eligible same-scope previous figures dated and visibly stale after a failed refresh. Do not treat an invalidated result as newly verified. A page load or successful connection does not prove financial completeness.
9. **Compare like with like.** The comparator needs its own compatible evidence, dates, currency, definitions and denominators. Missing comparison must not erase a valid current figure or create a trend claim.
10. **Separate arithmetic from cause.** A bridge can show which recorded components changed profit. It does not prove why customer behaviour changed, why an advert worked or whether a cost could be avoided.
11. **Separate profit and cash.** Profit, bank cash, operating cash and unsettled processor funds have different meanings. One must not stand in for another.
12. **Withhold unsupported advice.** No invented savings, confidence percentages, benchmarks, causal marketing returns or business-wide opportunity scores. Label conditional scenarios and their assumptions.
13. **Keep accounting sources distinct.** Shopify trading and Xero accounting results are not automatically matched, converted or reconciled. Explain their basis rather than forcing agreement.
14. **Do not act automatically.** A briefing, test plan or saved draft does not authorise payments, advertising changes, orders, external messages or reminders.
15. **Show the evidence.** An owner must be able to explore the relevant figures, calculations, dates and limitations behind a statement.

Approved recurring monthly overheads may be allocated by calendar day where finer data is unavailable, with an allocation label. This does not permit spreading every monthly sales, profit or cash figure into invented weekly/daily observations.

## 2. Recommended advice policy

**Proposed:** every briefing should answer four questions in everyday English: **What happened? What explains the arithmetic? What should I check or consider next? What limits that conclusion?** Put detailed calculations behind Explore the Numbers.

| Level | When allowed | How Night Scout should speak |
| --- | --- | --- |
| State a fact | The specific figure is supported. | “October contribution after advertising was £23,515.” |
| Explain a movement | Comparable evidence and a reconciled breakdown exist. | “Higher recorded fulfilment costs account for part of the reduction.” |
| Recommend an investigation | A supported observation warrants checking, but the business cause is unresolved. | “Review the fulfilment invoices and order mix before deciding what to change.” |
| Propose a bounded test | Baseline, assumptions, downside, measurement and owner decision are explicit. | “Consider a limited test, subject to cash, stock and service checks.” |
| Recommend a commercial change | Relevant economics, cash/commitments, capacity and evidence of the proposed mechanism support it. | Explain the evidence and alternatives; retain owner approval. Initially keep consequential recommendations under human review. |
| Hold back | The evidence cannot support the answer. | State the missing input and the next useful check. Do not fill the gap with confident prose. |

**Proposed common limits:** no universal “good CAC”, safe cash buffer, target margin, significance threshold or intervention size. Agree these for the business and version them. Show both £ and relative movement where meaningful; use percentage points for changes between rates. Avoid growth percentages from zero or negative baselines; explain the £ change instead. “Improved” describes the named measure, not the entire business. Seasonal comparisons need context; annualising one month is an illustration, not an annual forecast. Do not double-count overlapping opportunities.

| D&A — Agreed treatment | Identified depreciation and amortisation already included in overheads. | Add back once for EBITDA only. Excluding it does not remove the future need to replace assets. |

| EBITDA — Agreed; EBITDA margin — Current | Agreed EBITDA = operating profit + identified D&A. | Sales retained after discounts/refunds | Net product sales ÷ gross product sales. | Distinct from approved AOV and customer retention. Show discount and refund components. |
| All £ changes, growth rates and percentage-point changes | Matched current versus comparison amounts, with each period's own denominators. | State periods and units. Loss reduction is not automatically a meaningful positive growth percentage. |

Do not average the three per-order ratios without weighting. |

## 11. Proposed briefing style and examples

**Use:** a short headline, two or three supporting facts, one practical next check, and one relevant limitation. Make the business meaning visible within about ten seconds; keep a route to the calculations. Avoid a wall of caveats or a compulsory instruction where there is no justified action.

**Profit but falling cash:** “The business made a profit but used operating cash. The reconciliation shows supplier balances were the largest working-capital movement. Review the payments and balances behind that change before committing more cash. This does not establish how much is safe to spend.”

**Sales up, margin down:** “Sales increased, but less contribution remained per pound of revenue. Higher recorded discounts and delivery costs explain part of the arithmetic change. Check mix, promotions and delivery invoices before choosing a price or supplier change.”

**High ROAS, incomplete acquisition evidence:** “Sales were high relative to advertising spend. That includes repeat and non-advertising sales, so it does not prove another pound of advertising will be profitable. Check matched first-customer economics and cash capacity before considering a limited test.”

**Missing overheads:** “Sales and gross profit are supported. Business profit is unavailable because overhead evidence is incomplete. Complete that evidence before relying on a profit conclusion.”

## 12. Recorded decisions and remaining bounded choices

1. **Advice authority:** recommend investigations automatically; commercial changes initially remain conditional proposals with human review. Approve or amend the advice ladder.
2. **Business thresholds:** agree materiality, cash buffer, desired profit and any alert thresholds rather than adopting sample values. No numeric defaults are approved by this draft.
3. **Metric policies:** explicitly confirm product gross-margin and EBITDA-margin denominators; cash-burn edge cases; real-merchant identity/CAC/attribution/cohort/refund definitions. Existing synthetic implementation doesn't settle every merchant policy.
4. **Opportunity ranking:** decide whether to order investigations by transparent evidenced £ movement, separately from ranking commercial interventions by expected benefit/risk/effort.
5. **Test recommendations:** agree required downside checks, review/stop conditions and what would count as evidence of benefit.
6. **Future ChatGPT pilot:** separately agree permitted aggregate data, access/retention, model/request budget and activation. Never put customer identifiers or instructions embedded in source text into trusted financial reasoning. The AI writes within these rules; it does not change them.

Interpretation and advice safeguards are approved for implementation. Record unresolved exceptions explicitly and use page-by-page acceptance examples. This approval does not establish new financial formulas, numeric thresholds, model data processing or paid services.
